What stays, what goes

Kept, because it's you

  • Black stage, orange means "act". Orange shows up only for leave, due soon and the light.
  • Geist Mono. Labels, times and countdowns stay in small mono caps. That keeps the basement voice next to the new serif.
  • Halftone dots. They now make up the edge of the light, so the glow reads as printed, not like a stock gradient.
  • The departures board. One board on Work for the next thing out. It flips only when that thing changes.
  • The now sentence, live traffic, Canvas scores, weather and Spotify. All the data stays; it just lives in fewer places.

Changed or gone

  • Boxes and hairline grids become glass pills. Rows float on the dark instead of sitting in bordered panels.
  • The labeled dock becomes four fused circles with a white knob. Badges become one orange dot.
  • The Spotify bar goes away. It's an album circle up top that opens a sheet.
  • The weather sky and the moving glass effects go away. The light does that job now.
  • The summary line and the timeline on Today go away. They repeated Work and Schedule.
  • New type. Petrona for headlines and Outfit for text and buttons, your picks. Geist goes.
  • Start-up. Same idea as today's loading screen: the counter runs while the light ignites, then the screen rises in piece by piece. Once per session.
  • The light drifts. It moves slowly around the screen, one loop about every half minute. That's the only thing moving on its own.
  • Motion drops to a short list: the slow drift, the light breathing on "leave now", the knob sliding, and the board flipping on a change.

Do we need a new language?

No. Everything on this page is the same HTML and CSS your app already uses. It's written so it works on your iPhone right now, with no refraction required. Here are the options:

my pick

Stay on the web app

Same code and the same Cloudflare setup. It works on your iPhone, your Mac and claude.ai. It gets about 90% of this look. The only thing missing is Apple's real glass bending, which the redesign doesn't depend on.

Native iPhone app (SwiftUI)

This gets Apple's real Liquid Glass for free. But it's a new language, you'd have to reinstall it every 7 days unless you pay $99 a year for an Apple developer account, the Mac and claude.ai versions go away, and every feature gets rebuilt. It's worth it later only if you want widgets or a Lock Screen countdown.

Wrap the web app (Capacitor)

This makes it feel like an App Store app, but it's still Safari underneath, so it looks exactly like option 1. It's only worth it for push notifications or widgets.
